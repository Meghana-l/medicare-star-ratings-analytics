Medicare Advantage Star Ratings: Policy Analytics

Rebuilds the CMS Part C & D Star Ratings method from public data: raw measure scores, cut points, then stars. Includes gap-to-next-star analysis and data-quality checks.

How the stars are rebuilt

  1. Raw score. Each plan has a score per measure (for example, the share of diabetic members with a controlled blood sugar). Scores come from HEDIS clinical measures, CAHPS and HOS member surveys, plan-reported administrative measures and Part D drug measures.
  2. Cut points. For each measure, CMS publishes four thresholds that turn a score into 1 to 5 stars. For most measures a higher score is better. For a few (complaints, members leaving, readmissions) lower is better, so the thresholds run the other way.
  3. Measure star. The SQL view calc compares each score with the thresholds and assigns a star. The Data checks tab compares this with the star CMS published.
  4. Overall rating. The weighted average of measure stars, using each measure's weight, rounded to the nearest half star (the overall_calc view).
  5. Gap to next star. The gap_to_next view measures how far each score is from the next threshold.

What this does not do

  • The overall rating here is a weighted average of measure stars. CMS also applies a reward factor and other adjustments, so overall ratings can differ slightly. Measure-level stars are the exact check.
  • It works at the contract level, using published scores. It does not use member-level HEDIS data, which plans hold and NCQA specifies.
  • Boundary rules (whether a score equal to a cut point rounds up) follow the common convention and are covered by tests.
